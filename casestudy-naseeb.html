findnaseeb — In-Person Matchmaking

Case study · Co-founder · 2026–Present · findnaseeb.com

findnaseeb is an in-person matchmaking platform for the Muslim community. Instead of swiping through profiles, people come to real, curated events. I co-founded it with two others (one more engineer and a product/UX designer) and built the app: the web product, the APIs, the data model, and the AI step that helps decide who to invite to each event.

The problem

Most matchmaking apps optimize for swiping and endless profiles. They're easy to build and easy to ignore. We wanted the opposite: get compatible people into the same room. Which means the hard problem isn't the app, it's who's in the room. Curation is the product.

What I built

  • The web app and the APIs behind it, on Next.js and TypeScript.
  • The data model for members, events, and attendance.
  • The AI-assisted curation step: member profiles and event context (location, who's new, who's been before, group balance) go through an AI pass that drafts a guest list. A person always reviews it before anything goes out.
  • The member basics: profiles, the event flow, and safety (members can block anyone, and blocks come straight to us).

How curation works

A decision I'd defend. We let AI draft, but never decide. A bad match at a small in-person event is expensive, so a person reviews every guest list before invites go out. And with a three-person team, I kept the stack deliberately boring — Next.js and SQLite on a single box, easy to run and easy to reason about. We'll move off SQLite when real concurrency forces it, not before.

Where it's at

  • 200+ members in Calgary, grown with zero ad spend (organic Instagram and TikTok).
  • Waitlists open in 3 cities across 2 countries (Toronto, Vancouver, Chicago).
  • Every event has landed, because the guest lists are curated by hand, not thrown together by an algorithm.

What I learned

The unscalable part — hand-curating each event — is the part that works. AI makes it faster, but people keep the quality. And keeping the infrastructure boring is what lets a three-person team actually ship.
